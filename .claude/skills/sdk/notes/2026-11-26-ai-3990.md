Since AI-3990 the agent's scheduling tools wait on their step for the user to allow them (`schedule_task`, and a reschedule, snooze, resume or new instructions through `update_scheduled_task`), as a connector call under `require_approval` already did. Such a step is server-run: it comes as an `ai_tool_call` step with status `awaiting_approval` in the conversation, never as a `tool_call` frame, and is answered with `{"type": "tool_approval", "tool_call_id": <the step's id>, "request_id": <the reply's request_id>, "allowed": true|false}` on the session's events socket. Unanswered for 10 minutes, it is declined.

Go answers it with `Pipeline.AnswerApproval` (`sdks/go/stream/accelerated.go`), JS with `AgentChat.decide` (`decideShown`, `sdks/js/src/session.ts`) and Kotlin with `AgentSession.decide(ShownToolCall)`. Swift's `AgentSession.decide` (`sdks/swift/core/Sources/StreamAgents/AgentSession.swift`) and Dart's `AgentSession.decide` (`sdks/dart/stream_agents/lib/src/core/agent_session.dart`) answer only a client tool's call held in `approvals`, and return without sending anything for a server-run step, so an app built on them that shows the step, as StreamChatAI's approval card does, never reaches the router: the call waits 10 minutes and is declined. Each should send `tool_approval` for a step shown `awaiting_approval` that is not one of its own calls, as Kotlin does, and not run or answer a `tool_result` for it. The TUI (`tui/`) shows no approval at all. Ruby, Rust, PHP and .NET: check that their session clients can send `tool_approval` with a `request_id` and no `turn_id`.
